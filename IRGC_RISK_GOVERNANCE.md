# International Risk Governance Center (IRGC) AI Framework
## Risk Governance Architecture, Implementation Details, and Oversight Procedures

*Note: The canonical documentation file is also available at `/docs/IRGC_AI_RISK_GOVERNANCE_FRAMEWORK.md`.*

**Framework Identification:** IRGC Risk Governance Framework for Artificial Intelligence  
**Authoring Body:** International Risk Governance Center (EPFL, Lausanne, Switzerland)  
**Classification:** Systemic, Emerging & High-Uncertainty AI Risk Governance Standard  
**Target Audience:** Chief Risk Officers (CROs), AI Ethics Boards, Executive Governance Committees, Compliance Regulators, and Lead AI Architects

---

## Executive Summary

The IRGC framework provides an interdisciplinary architecture engineered specifically for complex, uncertain, and ambiguous AI risks. It structures risk governance into four progressive phases unified by a cross-cutting core of **Stakeholder Engagement**, **Communication**, and **Oversight Procedures**.

---

## The Four Core Phases

### Phase 1: Pre-Estimation (Framing, Scoping, and Early Warning)
- **Problem Framing & Scoping:** Define clear technical and organizational boundaries, identify potential dual-use failure modes, and establish baseline performance metrics.
- **Early Warning:** Proactive scanning of weak signals, emerging adversarial techniques, and international legislative changes.
- **Deliverable:** *Pre-Estimation AI Charter (IRGC-PE-01)*.

### Phase 2: Interdisciplinary Risk Appraisal
- **Scientific Risk Assessment:** Quantitative evaluation of prompt injection vulnerability, out-of-distribution robustness, systemic interdependencies, and bias metrics.
- **Concern Assessment:** Evaluation of public trust, ethical impacts, fundamental human rights, and socio-economic workforce disruptions.

### Phase 3: Risk Characterization and Evaluation
Categorize the AI system into one of the four distinct IRGC risk profiles:
1. **Simple Risks:** Deterministic hazards managed by Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs).
2. **Complex Risks:** High systemic interdependencies managed via extensive simulation and red teaming.
3. **Uncertain Risks:** Stochastic, non-deterministic behaviors managed by precautionary circuit breakers and low autonomy.
4. **Ambiguous Risks:** Competing ethical values managed via multi-stakeholder discourse and external ethics boards.
- **Tolerability Decision:** Classify system as *Acceptable*, *Tolerable with Mitigation (ALARP)*, or *Intolerable*.

### Phase 4: Risk Management Implementation
- **Risk Prevention:** Prohibit deployment of unconstrained or high-risk tasks.
- **Risk Mitigation:** Enforce technical guardrails (NeMo, Llama Guard), Human-in-the-Loop gates, and PII filters.
- **Risk Adaptation:** Real-time drift detection and automated rollback engines.
- **Risk Transfer:** Contractual indemnification and cyber/AI insurance.

---

## Cross-Cutting Oversight Procedures

- **AI Oversight Governance Board:** Multidisciplinary committee with unilateral veto power over model promotion.
- **RACI Governance Matrix:** Formally defined accountability between Executive Committee, AI Board, ML SecOps, and Legal.
- **Continuous Post-Market Audit:** Ongoing logging of model drift, adversarial attempts, and user feedback with annual recertification.
